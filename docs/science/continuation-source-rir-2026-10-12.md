## Scientific follow-up

Wiley Scholar Gateway · targeted effort/perception follow-up · 4 passages · 2 articles · 2025–2026. One article was relevant; three passages plus selected primary sections were reviewed. A broader five-article screen also included unrelated clinical/diet material, which was excluded from these app claims. Consensus screened nine records and fetched two original abstracts; it did not provide complete-paper review.

[Robinson et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2) provides exploratory support for interpreting proximity to failure differently across strength and hypertrophy, with estimated effort and uncertain exact relationships. It does not qualify a universal RIR target or this app's progression thresholds.

[Refalo et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021) reported similar quadriceps growth with failure and reserve-based work, with greater fatigue during failure work. The original abstract was fetched; full-paper review remains incomplete. The population was 18 trained adults performing two lower-body exercises.

[Refalo et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsc.12266) reports perceptual outcomes from the same intervention. Selected methods/results, Table 2 and limitations support individual feedback; long-term adherence was not established. This is not independent replication. The methods prose and analyzed sample table differ in the reported number of women; the abstract and table describe six women and twelve men. Figures and supplements were not independently verified.

The science library exposes review depth, and `docs/evidence-ledger.json` retains provenance, applicability and limitations. No new universal numerical scientific rule or exact-routine efficacy claim is qualified by this milestone. Per-set RIR remains optional user feedback; the ordinary app progression policy remains separately labeled.
