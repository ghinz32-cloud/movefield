# Shared fitness audit · 12 October 2026

Movefield and Grizzly use one canonical exercise library, planning engine, equipment survey, substitution policy and saved-data schema. Both web brands and both native source flavors consume the same changes. Existing accepted plans and workout records are retained.

## What was reviewed

`baseline-audit.json` records the original 989 exercise IDs and 71 definitions. `current-audit.json` records every current exercise and program separately, including instructions, logging conventions, source links, source scope, equipment, timing and movement coverage. This audit reuses the earlier movement-specific source reviews in `../catalog-audits/` and `../exercise-library-provenance.json`; the 866 imported source records were previously compared individually. Automated completeness does not replace reading or form assessment.

The library now has 995 entries: six explicitly named home variations were added with original instructions. The new entries keep their own histories. No existing exercise ID, multiplier or completed load record was repurposed. Six known core equipment labels and seven terminology gaps were corrected. Source photos remain intact and checksum verified. Every guide has understandable setup, movement, finish, breathing, easier-option, common-error, safety, recording and source sections. Form references are prominent on web and native, including the 112 guides whose sources were previously buried in disclosures.

One coach-selected sport-skill placeholder keeps its muscle targets unknown until a coach chooses the actual drill; it is not an automatically prescribed exercise. Twenty-seven imported machine variants still have unconfirmed loading conventions. They retain disabled load tracking/progression and are excluded from automatic inventory matches. Three old media keys are retained as legacy references; they are not newly prescribed exercises. A parent-movement or general reference is labeled as such; no new exact-variation video or live playback is claimed for the six additions.

## Evidence and its limits

The [ACSM 2026 public summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) was retrieved and read. It supports consistent individualized training, major-muscle work at least twice weekly, heavier resistance for strength, more weekly work for a growth emphasis, and viable band/bodyweight/home options. It reports approximately ten weekly sets per muscle group for greater hypertrophy emphasis. A public summary is not the full position stand: the publisher returned HTTP 402, and its review methods and certainty tables were not reviewed.

The full official [NSCA youth statement](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/position_stand_youth_resistance_training---2009.pdf) and [older-adult statement](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/2a4112fb355a4a48853bbafbe070fb8e/resistance_training_for_older_adults__position.1.pdf) were retrieved. Their relevant recommendations, population limits, program design and supervision/health sections were reviewed. Youth daily-habit options preserve two supervised nonconsecutive strength sessions. Older-adult access follows function, experience and preference; age does not invent starting weights or medical clearance.

All 27 [NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/) sequences match the retrieved official schedule. Added daily walking does not become another run or override the run prerequisites. App-authored short introductions and hybrid blocks remain explicitly distinct from the NHS sequence.

The evidence ledger records source hashes, access outcomes, review depths, claims and applicability. PubMed challenge pages were not treated as abstracts. Several full research papers remain inaccessible through this runtime; the user-linked Consensus and Wiley plugins have no callable tools in this session. Expert product pages do not validate numeric prescriptions or license copying paid programs. Exact sets, rep ranges, rest, calendar gaps, time estimates, rep progression and load safeguards remain transparent app decisions, not individually trial-validated recipes.

## Goal and preference decisions

Desired weekly training days are separate from available weekdays. The catalog includes six-day push/pull/lower splits, alternating SBD practice, very short practice, and daily activity habits; seven-day versions include easy activity. Weekly dose is distributed instead of repeating a full-body workout seven times. Six lifting days are an adult option; youth daily activity keeps supervised strength on two days.

The complete foundations were checked individually for chest/back/quadriceps/hamstring/glute work on at least two days. Missing second exposures were corrected with visible work and adequate time. Home plans use a band hinge where hamstring work is intended; a bridge remains a distinct glute-focused exercise. Short practice, no-equipment activity and SBD skill blocks keep their stated smaller scope. They do not promise equal results to fuller growth or strength blocks.

The plan preview shows regional involvement counts, strength/running/easy days and time. A compound set can involve several regions; the table does not equate indirect work with direct isolation sets. Low-dose starters, unsupported advanced calisthenics skills, sport-specific drills, race preparation and meet peaking remain clearly scoped.

The equipment survey records one dumbbell versus a matching pair, fixed versus adjustable weights, secure supports/anchors and each machine. Selected-stack and plate-loaded machines have distinct inventory choices. A confirmed survey is a closed list for one location. Full-gym branding does not imply every machine exists. Bodyweight alone does not silently become a loaded pulling program. Powerlifting retains actual barbell SBD; a machine-only gym gets an explicit fit failure rather than an invented equivalent.

Substitutes use curated movement roles, current inventory and the same logging metric. Their differences are visible. Changes preserve sets, rest, dates, past work and separate load histories; they invalidate stale previews. Entered active-workout data cannot be erased. Inventory edits are reviewed with the substitution and do not silently rewrite other workouts. Fixed dumbbells do not generate fictional heavier-load proposals.

The synthetic preference suite checks all nine goals, 2–7 desired days, six actual equipment profiles, two experience levels, six time windows and floor-access preferences. It verifies the accepted calendar, equipment fit, recovery, displayed dose, serialization, fixed-weight behavior, substitution semantics, youth supervision and all NHS timings. Validation receipts distinguish domain checks, browser interactions, native component simulations, platform exports and installed-device testing. A build or Android/iOS JavaScript export does not prove physical-device accessibility or App Store readiness.
